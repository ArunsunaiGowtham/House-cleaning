Move-Out Cleaning Guide Before Handing Over Your Keys

  • Diego Alvarez
  • 30 January 2026
  • 10 min read
  • Moving
  1. Home
  2. Move-Out Cleaning Guide Before Handing O

Deposit disputes are almost always about cleaning, not damage. Letting agents inspect against a written inventory, so the way to keep your money is to clean against the same list. Here is the room-by-room plan we use on every move-out job.

In this article

  1. Start with the inventory
  2. Kitchen: the deciding room
  3. Bathroom
  4. Living areas and bedrooms
  5. Floors and carpets
  6. Windows, frames and reveals
  7. Outside, storage and bins
  8. Final walkthrough and evidence

Start with the inventory

Print the check-in inventory and clean with it in your hand. It lists items an agent will photograph: extractor filters, window tracks, skirting boards, the inside of built-in wardrobes. Anything on that document is a potential deduction.

Kitchen: the deciding room

Oven interior, door glass, racks and trays. Extractor filter degreased. Fridge and freezer emptied, defrosted, wiped and left switched off with doors ajar. Cupboards emptied and wiped inside and out. Behind and beneath the appliances if they pull out safely.

Bathroom

Descale the shower screen, taps, showerhead and toilet base. Treat grout and silicone for mould. Clean the extractor cover, the underside of the toilet seat and the cistern top. Remove every consumable and drain the bathroom bin.

“Agents do not deduct for a home that looks clean. They deduct for the six items on the inventory nobody checked.”

Living areas and bedrooms

Dust and wipe every skirting board, door, door frame, switch plate and radiator including the fins. Clean the inside of all built-in storage. Remove picture hooks and fill holes only if your tenancy agreement requires it.

Floors and carpets

Vacuum thoroughly, then treat carpets with hot-water extraction. Many contracts require professional carpet cleaning with a receipt, so check your clause before you decide to hire a machine yourself.

Windows, frames and reveals

Interior glass, frames, sills and the window tracks where dirt collects. Tracks are one of the most commonly deducted items because they are the easiest thing to forget.

Outside, storage and bins

Sweep balconies and patios, clear the garden of rubbish, wash out the bins, and empty every loft, shed or garage space. Anything left behind can be charged as a removal cost.

Final walkthrough and evidence

Walk the property in daylight with the inventory, then photograph every room, appliance interior and floor with a timestamp. If a deduction is proposed later, dated photographs settle it quickly.

Quick checklist

  • Oven and extractor degreased
  • Fridge defrosted and left open
  • Limescale removed throughout
  • Skirting and window tracks cleaned
  • Carpets professionally extracted
  • Timestamped photos of every room
